Compliance, In Plain Language

The part worth sitting with.

Take your time here. This is the material we most want you to genuinely understand — because trust is the foundation of everything your practice does, and we take it as seriously as you do. Expand any question below.

What is a BAA?
  • A Business Associate Agreement — the contract HIPAA requires between a healthcare provider and any vendor who handles patient data on their behalf. It legally obligates the vendor to protect that data and defines who’s responsible if something goes wrong.
Why does this matter more for a therapy practice?
  • Psychotherapy notes receive extra legal protection beyond standard medical records — requiring specific patient authorization even for billing use.
  • If substance-use treatment is ever involved, federal 42 CFR Part 2 rules stack on top of HIPAA with even stricter requirements and potential criminal penalties.
  • Courts and regulators treat behavioral-health data as higher-sensitivity than most other medical categories, because exposure can affect employment, custody, and relationships.
How common is this gap, really?
  • Small medical & dental practices accounted for 55% of OCR financial penalties in 2022 — being small does not mean being overlooked.
  • Over 60% of small healthcare providers name HIPAA compliance as a major ongoing challenge.
  • Third-party vendor involvement in healthcare breaches roughly doubled in 2025 (15% → 30%) — the fastest-growing breach category, and exactly the “no BAA with your CRM/forms vendor” scenario.
  • Most common violations behind fines: lack of risk analysis (71%), insufficient access controls (54%), failure to encrypt (48%).
  • Real settlements at small-practice scale: a treatment center fined $103,000 for missing basic safeguards; another practice settled for $10,000 over an impermissible disclosure.
  • The math: OCR counts every day PHI is handled without a signed BAA as a separate violation. For 100 patients over a year, that’s 36,500 distinct violations — which is why getting it right up front matters. We surface this to build confidence, not to alarm.
What does GoHighLevel’s HIPAA add-on actually include?
  • A signed BAA between GoHighLevel and the account holder.
  • AES-256 encryption of data at rest.
  • Mandatory multi-factor authentication.
  • Audit logging of who accessed what, and when.
  • Restricted platform-support access. It’s a permanent, non-reversible setting once enabled — not toggled on for a phase and off later.
What does ZAH Brand Solutions’ own BAA cover?
  • It’s a separate agreement directly between ZAH and New Vision — because ZAH has access to the system (and potentially PHI) while building and managing it. GoHighLevel’s BAA covers GoHighLevel’s own infrastructure; it does not cover the agency working inside your account. Ours does.
